Do not write speaker notes, spoken lines, or scripts in the user's voice. After about ten rejected drafts (flat, staccato, run-on, hats trope, "technically true" thread, sales tone), the user said the writing is a mockery of their style and asked for a division of labor: Claude describes each slide, the logical build-up, and the props; the user writes every spoken word.

**Why:** matching their rhythm and stance from a sample ("From tree to graph" notes) failed repeatedly; the voice cannot be pattern-matched. Also retired: the "hats" trope (theoretical linguist and so on) and the "technically true" through-line, both used in earlier talks.
**How to apply:** for talks, produce slide descriptions (job, on-slide text, props, facts to keep true) and help with the argument and the visuals. Slide labels and titles are fine; spoken prose is not. Do not argue about it. The talk is the three-ad detergent shape (stain, why, product, clean result, tagline) with the red thread "how to fix things"; see [[project-lightning-talk]]. Stage constraint: in person, no notes visible, so lines must be memorizable.
